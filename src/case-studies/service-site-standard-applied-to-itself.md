## Why this is the first case study

Most case studies compare a before and an after. This one has no before. It's the first website Pikes Peak Web Designs launched, built from a blank file to show the Service Site Standard from end to end before any client paid for it.

So instead of "I made a slow site fast," this study says "I built it right the first time, and you can check every claim with the same free tools I use."

## The build, principle by principle

Every site I build follows the seven principles of [the Service Site Standard](/method/). Here's how each shows up on this one.

### Principle 1: One owner, end to end

I wrote every page, style rule, form, and script on this site. No subcontractor, partner, or offshore developer worked on any of it.

### Principle 2: Custom code over CMSes

The site is plain, hand-written files, with no content management system (the editing platform most sites run on) and no website builder underneath. View the source of any page to check.

### Principle 3: Posted prices, posted process

Every detail of working together is public: [pricing](/pricing/), the [service agreement](/agreement/), the [owner's guide](/owners-guide/), the [technical approach](/technical-approach/), and the [Service Site Standard](/method/). The call confirms fit instead of revealing the deal.

### Principle 4: Fast by construction

Every page scores 95 to 100 on Google's speed test on phones. The first data arrives in well under a tenth of a second, images come in several sizes, and each page loads only the styles it uses. None of that was added later.

### Principle 5: Accessible by default

Every page meets WCAG 2.2 AA, with checked contrast, buttons large enough to tap, full keyboard use, and visible focus outlines. The [accessibility statement](/accessibility/) is a commitment backed by the agreement.

### Principle 6: Local before global

My business depends less on Google's map results (the "local pack") than a typical service business, but the site is built the same way: business labels on the home page, matching contact details on every page, city pages for the metros I serve, and maps with no Google tracking.

### Principle 7: Boring infrastructure

Every tool under this site is long established and has changed little in three years, so the site will run the same way in 2030. The [technical approach](/technical-approach/) names each tool.

## What's on the site

- **The main pages.** Home, pricing, how it works, what I build, the Standard, technical approach, samples, sample forms, and contact.
- **Eight comparisons** with the common alternatives, from website builders to agencies and freelancers.
- **Six live demo sites** for roofing, home inspection, landscaping, HVAC, electrical, and plumbing, each with its own look and a working contact form.
- **A 165-entry glossary** of every technical term on the site, in plain words.
- **Service-area pages for 30 metros** across the U.S. and Canada, each with a local map and local content.
- **A client portal** with forms for onboarding, content updates, design feedback, emergencies, referrals, and testimonials.
- **The working documents:** the service agreement, owner's guide, accessibility statement, privacy policy, and terms of use.

That's more than 100 pages. You can test any of them in [Google's speed test](https://pagespeed.web.dev), and all of it is built the same way as the $175-a-month plan I offer clients.

## What this proves

If my own site couldn't meet the Standard, the Standard would be just talk. It does, and anyone with a browser can check every claim on this page today.
