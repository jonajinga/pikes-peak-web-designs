<p>Your Google Business Profile, the free listing that puts you in Google's map results (the "local pack"), is the biggest source of unpaid search traffic for most service businesses. It's also the most neglected. Owners set it up once and forget it, while Google <em>specifically</em> rewards profiles that show recent activity.</p>

<p>It takes four tasks and 15 minutes on the first day of each quarter. This is the list I give every client, whatever the trade or city.</p>

<h2>1. Confirm your hours are current</h2>

<p>Wrong hours are the most common reason businesses lose calls. A customer who finds a locked door when Google said "open" leaves a 1-star review, and one who skips calling because Google said "closed" never reaches you at all.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Check that your regular weekly hours are still right.</li>
  <li>Add holiday hours for the next 90 days, at least for Christmas, New Year's, July 4th, Thanksgiving, Memorial Day, and Labor Day. Enter "closed" or the changed hours instead of leaving Google to guess.</li>
  <li>If you offer emergency or 24-hour service, set "open 24 hours" or fill in the after-hours phone field.</li>
  <li>If you close for a season, set those dates in advance.</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. Add one to three fresh photos</h2>

<p>Google ranks recently updated profiles higher, and new photos are the easiest update. They needn't be professional, only recent.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Job photos.</strong> Before-and-after shots of recent work, even from a phone.</li>
  <li><strong>Crew photos.</strong> The crew, the truck, the shop, so searchers see the people behind the business.</li>
  <li><strong>Location photos.</strong> If customers visit you, show them what to look for from the street.</li>
</ul>

<p>Three photos a quarter is 12 a year. Most competitors upload none.</p>

<h2>3. Reply to every review older than two weeks</h2>

<p>Google ranks profiles with replies above quiet ones, and customers comparing two contractors weigh reviews more than anything else. Each reply shows both that you're paying attention.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>5-star reviews.</strong> A short thank-you that mentions something specific, in two sentences at most.</li>
  <li><strong>4-star reviews.</strong> Thank them and answer the concern in public: "Thanks for the feedback. We've changed how we schedule since your visit, so the wait should be shorter next time."</li>
  <li><strong>3-star or lower.</strong> Reply politely, offer to sort it out privately ("I'd like to make this right. Can you reach me at [phone/email]?"), and never argue. Future customers read your reply more closely than the complaint.</li>
  <li><strong>Untruthful reviews.</strong> Flag them for removal. If Google won't remove one, reply once, calmly, and move on.</li>
</ul>

<p>After catching up, answer new reviews within 48 hours.</p>

<h2>4. Post one update or offer</h2>

<p>The "Posts" tab is the profile's most underused feature. Posts are free, appear in your search listing, and show Google an active business, yet most owners forget they exist.</p>

<p>Post anything legitimate: a seasonal service ("furnace tune-ups starting October 1"), a recent job ("just finished a 50kW commercial solar install in [city]"), an offer ("10% off for first-time customers"), or news ("we just earned [certification]"). Aim for once a quarter at least, and once a month if you can.</p>

<h2>Four tasks in 15 minutes</h2>

<p>The full checklist:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Confirm your hours and add the next 90 days of holiday hours.</li>
  <li>Upload one to three fresh photos.</li>
  <li>Reply to every review older than two weeks.</li>
  <li>Post one update or offer.</li>
</ol>

<p>Set a reminder for the first Monday of January, April, July, and October. Within a year, your profile will be one of the most active in your trade and area, which is what Google looks for.</p>

<p>It helps even more when your website matches the profile: the same name, address, phone, and hours, plus structured data (code that states those details for Google). Most sites and profiles disagree somewhere, and the fix is usually simple.</p>
