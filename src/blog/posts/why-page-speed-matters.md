<p>Most contractors focus on how their website looks: the colors, the fonts, and whether the photos look professional. Those things matter, but another factor has a more direct effect on whether your site brings in leads, and most owners never think about it.</p>

<p>That factor is page speed, or how fast your website loads.</p>

<p>The gap between a fast site and a slow one can decide whether a customer calls you or your competitor.</p>

<h2>The Numbers Are Blunt</h2>

<p>Google's own research has shown that as a page takes longer to load, its bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave without doing anything) rises sharply:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A page that loads in 1 second has a bounce rate around 9%</li>
  <li>At 3 seconds, that rate jumps to 32%</li>
  <li>At 5 seconds, you're losing more than half your visitors</li>
  <li>At 10 seconds, the chance a visitor leaves is more than 120% higher than at 1 second</li>
</ul>

<p>Think about what that means. If 100 people visit your site and it takes five seconds to load, more than half of them leave before they see your phone number, a single review, or even what you do.</p>

<p>All of those people found you on Google, and you lost them anyway.</p>

<h2>Speed Is a Google Ranking Factor</h2>

<p>Speed also affects whether people find you at all. Google uses page speed as a direct ranking signal, both in regular search results and in its map results (the local pack). A slow site ranks lower than an otherwise identical fast one.</p>

<p>In 2021, Google made Core Web Vitals an official ranking factor. They measure how fast a page loads, how quickly it responds, and how steady the layout stays while content loads. Sites that score well get a ranking boost, and sites that score poorly are pushed down.</p>

<p>Most contractor websites built on WordPress, with several plugins, large images, and generic themes, score poorly. Scores of 30 to 50 out of 100 are common.</p>

<h2>What Makes Websites Slow</h2>

<p>Slow websites usually share the same causes:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Large, unoptimized images.</strong> A photo from your phone is often 4 to 8 MB, but on a web page it should be under 200 KB. Images that haven't been resized or compressed are the biggest cause of slow pages.</li>
  <li><strong>Too many plugins.</strong> WordPress sites often run 15 to 30 plugins, and each one adds style code, scripts, and database lookups. Every plugin adds weight.</li>
  <li><strong>Heavy themes.</strong> Page-builder themes such as Divi or Elementor load hundreds of kilobytes of code whether or not you use their features.</li>
  <li><strong>Slow hosting.</strong> Budget shared hosting puts your site on a server with hundreds of others. When that server is busy, your pages slow down.</li>
  <li><strong>No caching.</strong> Without caching, every visit makes the server look everything up and build the page from scratch. With caching, most visitors get a ready-made copy that loads almost instantly.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What a Fast Website Looks Like</h2>

<p>Sites I build consistently score 95 to 100 on Google's PageSpeed Insights. Here's why:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Custom-coded HTML and CSS, with no frameworks and no unused code</li>
  <li>Images compressed and served in modern formats (WebP)</li>
  <li>No plugins, page builders, or WordPress overhead</li>
  <li>Fast hosting, with pages delivered from a CDN (a network of servers around the world)</li>
  <li>Only the JavaScript each page needs</li>
</ul>

<p>The result is a site that lands in the green on Core Web Vitals on every page. Google rewards that, and your competitors aren't offering it.</p>

<h2>How to Check Your Current Speed</h2>

<p>Go to PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev) and enter your website address. Google gives you a score from 0 to 100 and tells you exactly what's slowing you down. Use the mobile tab, since mobile scores are usually much lower than desktop scores and most of your visitors are on phones.</p>

<p>If your score is below 70, you have a significant problem that's hurting your search ranking and costing you leads. Below 50 is a serious problem to fix right away.</p>

<h2>The Appearance vs. Speed Trade-Off</h2>

<p>A fast website doesn't have to look bare or cheap. The sites I build are polished and professional; they just don't carry the weight of a WordPress installation underneath.</p>

<p>Many contractors choose an impressive-looking template with animations, scrolling effects, and video backgrounds without knowing what those features cost in speed. They make a site look like 2015, and they make it load slowly.</p>

<p>Clean design, clear content, fast pages, and a prominent phone number will turn more visitors into calls than any flashy template. You can measure the difference in call volume.</p>
