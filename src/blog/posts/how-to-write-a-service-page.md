<p>A service page has two jobs. It has to rank on Google so the right people find it, and it has to convince them to pick up the phone. Most service pages fail at one or both.</p>

<p>Here's a structure that works for search engines and for the people deciding whether to call you.</p>

<h2>Start With the Right Page Title</h2>

<p>Your page title, the text in the browser tab and in Google's results, is the most important search element on the page. It should be specific and name both the service and the location. Avoid titles like these:</p>
<ul>
  <li>"Services" ❌</li>
  <li>"Plumbing Services" ❌</li>
</ul>

<p>Use titles like these instead:</p>
<ul>
  <li>"Water Heater Installation &amp; Repair in [City] | [Your Company]" ✓</li>
  <li>"Emergency HVAC Repair in [City]: Same-Day Service | [Your Company]" ✓</li>
</ul>

<p>Specific beats generic. The service and the city in the title are the minimum for local search.</p>

<h2>The H1 Headline: Address the Customer's Need</h2>

<p>The H1, the main headline on the page, should match what the searcher wants, not only name the service. Someone searching "AC not working" isn't thinking "air conditioning repair." They're thinking, "My house is hot, and I need this fixed today."</p>

<p>Compare:</p>
<ul>
  <li>"Air Conditioning Repair Services" (adequate)</li>
  <li>"Fast AC Repair in [City]: Most Jobs Same Day" (speaks to the concern)</li>
</ul>

<p>The second version recognizes the urgency and answers the unspoken question: "Will you fix it quickly?"</p>

<h2>The Opening Paragraph: Answer "Can You Help Me?"</h2>

<p>The first paragraph under the headline should quickly confirm that the visitor is in the right place. Start with their situation instead of your company history:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "When your air conditioner stops working in the middle of summer, you need a technician fast, not a voicemail and a three-day wait. We handle AC repair throughout [City] and the surrounding area, with same-day service available for most calls."
</blockquote>

<p>In two sentences, you've confirmed the service and the location and answered the worry about speed.</p>

<h2>List Each Specific Service</h2>

<p>A page about "plumbing" is weak. A page about "water heater installation" that names the brands you service (Rheem, Bradford White, A.O. Smith), the types you install (tank, tankless, heat pump), and the steps you follow has substance.</p>

<p>Google rewards detail, because detailed pages match detailed searches. A homeowner searching "tankless water heater installation [city]" will find a page that says "tankless water heater installation" far more easily than a general plumbing page that buries it in a list.</p>

<p>A good service page follows this order:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Opening statement (what you do, where, and how fast)</li>
  <li>Why the service matters, or the common problems you solve</li>
  <li>A specific list of what the service includes</li>
  <li>Your process, step by step</li>
  <li>Trust signals (licensing, insurance, certifications)</li>
  <li>Customer reviews of this service</li>
  <li>FAQ section</li>
  <li>Call to action</li>
</ol>

<h2>Include a FAQ Section</h2>

<p>An FAQ section does two jobs. For customers, it answers the questions they have before calling, which eases hesitation and screens out poor fits. For Google, FAQ content often ranks for informational searches and can appear as expanded results (rich snippets).</p>

<p>Good FAQ questions for a plumbing service page:</p>
<ul>
  <li>How long does a water heater replacement take?</li>
  <li>Do you haul away the old unit?</li>
  <li>What brands do you recommend?</li>
  <li>Is a tankless water heater worth it?</li>
  <li>Do you offer financing?</li>
</ul>

<p>Answering these common questions on the page removes doubts and makes your site more useful than competitors' sites.</p>

<h2>Trust Signals on Every Service Page</h2>

<p>Many visitors arrive on a service page straight from search and never see your homepage, so don't count on the trust signals you put there.</p>

<p>Every service page should include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Your license number or "Licensed &amp; Insured," displayed prominently</li>
  <li>At least two or three customer reviews of the service</li>
  <li>Years in business or number of jobs completed</li>
  <li>A line confirming your service area ("Serving [City] and surrounding communities")</li>
</ul>

<h2>Call to Action: Make It Obvious</h2>

<p>End every service page with a clear, specific call to action instead of a vague "contact us for more information":</p>

<ul>
  <li>"Call us at [number] for a free estimate. Most quotes take five minutes over the phone."</li>
  <li>"Request your free inspection below, and we'll call you back within two hours."</li>
</ul>

<p>Tell visitors what to do, what happens next, and why to act now. The easier and clearer the next step, the more people take it.</p>

<h2>Length: How Long Should a Service Page Be?</h2>

<p>Long enough to be useful, and short enough that people keep reading. For most service pages, aim for 500 to 900 words of solid information about the service and your qualifications, without padding.</p>

<p>Google rewards substance. A 200-word service page is thin by today's standards, and a 2,000-word page full of repetition is weak too, so aim for quality and completeness rather than a word count.</p>
