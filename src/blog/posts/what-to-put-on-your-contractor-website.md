<p>One of the most common mistakes I see on contractor websites is saying too much, or saying the wrong things. A homeowner looking for a plumber or an electrician doesn't want to read an essay. They want three answers as fast as possible: Can you help me? Can I trust you? How do I reach you?</p>

<p>Your website's job is to answer those three questions and get out of the way.</p>

<p>Here's what belongs on a contractor or service business website, and what you can safely leave off.</p>

<h2>What to Include</h2>

<h3>A Clear, Specific Headline</h3>
<p>The top section of your home page is the first thing visitors see, and it needs a headline that says exactly what you do and where. "Quality Service You Can Trust" says nothing. Try something like:</p>
<ul>
  <li><em>"Licensed Roofing Contractor Serving Colorado Springs and El Paso County"</em></li>
  <li><em>"HVAC Installation and Repair in Monument, Fountain, and Woodland Park"</em></li>
  <li><em>"Colorado Springs Plumbing, Available 24/7 for Emergencies"</em></li>
</ul>
<p>Specific beats generic every time.</p>

<h3>Your Phone Number, on Every Page</h3>
<p>Put it in the header, on the home page, on the contact page, and in the footer, wherever a customer might decide to call. On phones, it should be a link that opens the dialer with one tap.</p>

<h3>A List of Your Specific Services</h3>
<p>Don't just say "we do plumbing." List the services you offer, such as water heater installation, sewer line repair, leak detection, bathroom remodels, and emergency shutoffs. The more specific you are, the better Google understands your site, and the more likely you are to rank for those searches.</p>

<h3>Your Service Area</h3>
<p>Name the cities, communities, and ZIP codes you work in. Colorado Springs is obvious, but what about Fountain, Manitou Springs, Monument, Woodland Park, or Black Forest? The more specific your list, the better you'll rank in those local searches, and the fewer calls you'll get from outside your area.</p>

<h3>Photos of Your Own Work</h3>
<p>Skip the stock photos and clip art. Show before-and-after shots of jobs you've finished, plus your truck, your crew, and your equipment. They don't need to be professional photos; clear phone pictures are fine. Photos of your own work build trust in a way stock images can't.</p>

<h3>Proof of Legitimacy</h3>
<p>Show your license number, insurance status, certifications or manufacturer authorizations, BBB membership if you have it, and years in business. These matter a great deal to homeowners who are inviting a stranger into their home.</p>

<h3>Customer Reviews</h3>
<p>Even three or four testimonials make a noticeable difference. If you have strong Google or Yelp reviews, show them on your site. Reviews from other customers are one of the most persuasive things you can include, and they cost nothing.</p>

<h3>A Simple Contact Form</h3>
<p>Not everyone wants to call. Some people would rather send a quick message and wait for a reply. A short form with name, email, phone, and a brief description of the project is enough, because the longer the form, the fewer people fill it out.</p>

<h2>What to Skip</h2>

<h3>Long Company History Essays</h3>
<p>Nobody reads five paragraphs about your grandfather starting the business in 1978. A sentence or two about how long you've been in business and what sets you apart is plenty. Save the longer story for your About page, and even there, keep it focused on what matters to the customer.</p>

<h3>Autoplaying Music or Videos</h3>
<p>This should go without saying, but it still happens. If your site plays sound on its own, people leave right away. If you want a video, mute it by default and let visitors start it.</p>

<h3>Pop-Up Offers</h3>
<p>Pop-ups that cover the page within two seconds annoy visitors and work against service businesses. If you have a promotion, put it in the top section of the page or in a banner, not in a pop-up.</p>

<h3>Cluttered Navigation</h3>
<p>If your main menu has more than seven items, start cutting. Most service business sites need four to six pages: Home, Services, About, Service Area, Gallery or Portfolio, and Contact. The simpler the menu, the more likely visitors are to find what they need and act on it.</p>

<h3>Testimonials From 2012</h3>
<p>If your newest review is several years old, it works against you. It suggests you're either not getting new customers or not paying attention to your website. Keep reviews current by adding new ones as they come in.</p>

<h2>One Final Rule: Make Every Page Answer "Why You?"</h2>

<p>Every page on your site should make a visitor feel you're the right contractor for the job: not just any contractor, but you. That means being specific about your experience, your coverage area, the quality of your work, and your reliability.</p>

<p>Generic websites get ignored. Specific, credible, well-organized websites get calls.</p>

<p>If you're not sure your current site does that, take 10 minutes to look at it the way a homeowner would: someone who's never heard of you and is deciding in 30 seconds whether to call. What do they see?</p>
