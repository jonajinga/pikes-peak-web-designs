<p>A home buyer who needs an inspection is usually on a tight deadline. The purchase agreement has a date on it, so they search right away, often on a phone and often the same day they make an offer. If your website doesn't show up, load fast, and make booking obvious, they'll call someone else.</p>

<p>Here's what an inspector's website needs to turn those searches into bookings.</p>

<h2>Your Service Area Has to Be Clear Immediately</h2>

<p>Buyers first need to know whether you serve their area, and they won't dig through an "About" page to find out. If your city, county, or service radius isn't at the top of the page, many will leave within seconds.</p>

<p>Be specific. "Serving Denver and the surrounding metro area" is better than nothing, but "Serving Denver, Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, and surrounding Jefferson and Arapahoe counties" tells a buyer exactly where you work. The detail also helps your local search rankings.</p>

<h2>Make Booking as Easy as Possible</h2>

<p>Buyers under contract are motivated. They want to lock in a time, not fill out a form and wait for a callback. At a minimum, your site should offer:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A phone number at the top of every page</li>
  <li>A direct link to your scheduling system (such as ISN, HomeGauge Scheduler, or Calendly)</li>
  <li>A simple inquiry form as an after-hours backup</li>
</ul>

<p>Every extra step is a chance to lose them, and the easier scheduling is, the more inspections you'll book from the same traffic.</p>

<h2>Certifications and Credentials Build Instant Trust</h2>

<p>In home inspection, credentials carry weight. Buyers and their agents are trusting you with a lot, since a missed defect can mean costly problems after closing.</p>

<p>Show your credentials up front, not tucked into a bio:</p>
<ul>
  <li>ASHI (American Society of Home Inspectors) or InterNACHI membership and certification</li>
  <li>State license number</li>
  <li>Years of experience and number of inspections completed</li>
  <li>Specialty certifications, such as radon, mold, sewer scope, and thermal imaging</li>
</ul>

<p>If you're InterNACHI certified, that logo on your homepage means something to buyers and agents who know it. Put it where they'll see it.</p>

<h2>Separate Pages for Each Inspection Type</h2>

<p>Most inspectors offer more than a standard buyer's inspection: radon testing, sewer scope, pre-listing inspections, new-construction phase inspections, and 11-month warranty inspections. People search for each one by name.</p>

<p>A page for each service lets you speak to that client's situation, answer their questions, and rank for the words they search. One "Services" page with a bulleted list serves those searches far less well.</p>

<h2>Write for Agents as Well as Buyers</h2>

<p>Buyers sometimes find their own inspector, but much of the work comes from real estate agent referrals. Agents refer inspectors they trust: people who communicate well, show up on time, write thorough reports, and don't sink deals over minor issues.</p>

<p>A short section or page for agents, covering report turnaround, how you communicate, and the property types you know, can be what puts you on an agent's referral list.</p>

<h2>Reviews Are Your Most Important Asset</h2>

<p>Buyers can't judge an inspection before they hire you, so they rely on what others say. A strong set of Google reviews is the most credible thing on your website, because buyers know reviews are hard to fake.</p>

<p>Show your star rating and review count where people will see them, and link to your Google Business Profile. Feature reviews that praise your thoroughness, your communication, or your skill with a certain property type, and place them where buyers decide, usually within the first screen or two.</p>

<h2>Report Samples Show Quality Before the Hire</h2>

<p>One of the most effective things your site can show is a sample report. Buyers and agents want to know what they'll get, and a clear, thorough sample with photos proves your professionalism before anyone calls.</p>

<p>It needn't be a full 80-page report. An excerpt showing how you document findings, mark up photos, and explain recommendations sets you apart from inspectors whose reports are a vague checklist.</p>

<h2>Speed Matters When Buyers Are Deciding in Real Time</h2>

<p>A buyer searching on a phone after making an offer has no patience for a slow site. If yours takes more than a couple of seconds to load, they've moved on to the next result, so a fast, phone-friendly site decides whether you get the call.</p>

<p>The inspectors with full calendars show up early in local search and make booking easy the moment a buyer arrives. A well-built website gives you that visibility, speed, and easy scheduling.</p>
