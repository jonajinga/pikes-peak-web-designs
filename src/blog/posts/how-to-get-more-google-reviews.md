<p>Google reviews are the closest thing to free advertising a service business has. A steady stream of 5-star reviews will do more for your phone than most paid campaigns, and unlike ads, their value builds over time.</p>

<p>Most contractors don't have a satisfaction problem. They do good work, and their customers are happy. But happy customers don't leave reviews on their own; you have to ask.</p>

<p>Here's a simple system that works.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Get Your Direct Review Link</h2>

<p>Before you ask, get your Google review link, the address that takes customers straight to the review box without searching for your business. Removing that step makes far more people follow through.</p>

<p>To find it, search for your business on Google, open your Business Profile, and click "Get more reviews" (or "Share review form") in the dashboard. Copy the link for your texts and emails.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Ask at the Right Moment</h2>

<p>Ask right after you finish a job, while you're still on site or within a few hours. That's when the customer is happiest and the work is fresh.</p>

<p>In person, you can say something like:</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "We appreciate your business. If you're happy with the work, a quick Google review would mean a lot to us. I'll text you a direct link right now."
</blockquote>

<p>Then send it immediately, before you head back to the office. The longer you wait, the fewer people respond.</p>

<h2>Step 3: The Follow-Up Text Template</h2>

<p>Keep it short, because nobody reads a long message asking for a review. Something like:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Your Company]. If you have two minutes, we'd appreciate a Google review. It helps us grow. Here's a direct link: [your review URL]. Thanks again."
</blockquote>

<p>That's all: a short, warm note with the link up front, and no long explanation or pleading.</p>

<h2>What If They Don't Respond?</h2>

<p>One follow-up, three to five days later, is fine. Keep it brief:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Hi [Name], just following up. If you have a chance to leave a Google review, we'd appreciate it. [link]"
</blockquote>

<p>After two tries, let it go. You want reviews from customers who are glad to give them, and your follow-up should feel like a friendly reminder.</p>

<h2>Respond to Every Review</h2>

<p>Most contractors skip this, and it costs them twice.</p>

<p>First, replying shows Google you're an active, engaged business, which is a small ranking factor in local search.</p>

<p>Second, potential customers read your replies. A thoughtful reply to a good review builds trust, and a calm, professional reply to a bad one can win over customers who were on the fence.</p>

<p>For positive reviews, a short personal reply works:</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Thank you, [Name]. It was a pleasure working on your [project]. We appreciate you taking the time to share your experience. Call us anytime."
</blockquote>

<p>For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge the concern, and offer to make it right privately. Never argue in public.</p>

<h2>Display Reviews on Your Website</h2>

<p>Feature your best Google reviews on your website too. Copy three to five onto your homepage or a testimonials page. A live-updating widget isn't needed; the customer's name and a star graphic beside each review is enough.</p>

<p>Reviews on your site reach visitors who are already deciding whether to call. The more proof from other customers they see before they pick up the phone, the more of them call.</p>

<h2>The Compound Effect</h2>

<p>Reviews build on themselves. Once you have 20 or more recent, solid reviews, people see you as the obvious choice because of what other customers say. That's worth more than any ad you can buy.</p>

<p>With that many reviews, Google also ranks you higher for more searches. That brings more customers, who leave more reviews, and the cycle feeds itself.</p>

<p>It starts with one ask. Make asking a habit after every job, and your review count will grow steadily without much thought.</p>
