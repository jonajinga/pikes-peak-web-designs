<p>Google reviews are the closest thing to free advertising that exists for a service business. A steady stream of genuine 5-star reviews will do more for your phone volume than most paid marketing campaigns, and unlike ads, the value compounds over time.</p>

<p>The problem most contractors have isn't customer satisfaction. Most of them do good work and their customers are happy. The problem is that happy customers don't leave reviews automatically. You have to ask.</p>

<p>Here's a simple system that works.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Get Your Direct Review Link</h2>

<p>Before you can ask for reviews, make sure you have your Google review link; the direct URL that takes customers straight to the review box without making them search for your business. This removes friction and dramatically increases follow-through.</p>

<p>To get it: Search for your business on Google, click on your Business Profile, then click "Get more reviews" (or "Share review form") in the dashboard. Copy that link. You'll use it in your text messages and emails.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Ask at the Right Moment</h2>

<p>The right moment is right after you finish a job, while you're still on-site or within a few hours of completing the work. That's when the customer's satisfaction is highest and the experience is fresh.</p>

<p>In person, you can say something like:</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "We really appreciate your business. If you're happy with the work, a quick Google review would mean a lot to us; I'll text you a direct link right now."
</blockquote>

<p>Then send it immediately. Don't wait until you get back to the office. The longer you wait, the lower the response rate.</p>

<h2>Step 3: The Follow-Up Text Template</h2>

<p>Keep it short. Nobody reads a long message asking for a review. Something like:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Your Company]! If you have 2 minutes, we'd really appreciate a Google review; it helps us grow. Here's a direct link: [your review URL]. Thanks again!"
</blockquote>

<p>That's it. No lengthy explanation, no begging, no multiple paragraphs. Short, warm, direct, with the link front and center.</p>

<h2>What If They Don't Respond?</h2>

<p>One follow-up, three to five days later, is acceptable. Something brief:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Hey [Name], just wanted to follow up; if you had a chance to leave a Google review, we really appreciate it! [link]"
</blockquote>

<p>After two attempts, drop it. Don't harass customers. You want genuine reviews from people who are actually happy, and you want your follow-up to feel like a friendly reminder, not a pest.</p>

<h2>Respond to Every Review</h2>

<p>This is something most contractors skip, and it's a missed opportunity on two fronts.</p>

<p>First, responding to reviews is a signal to Google that you're an active, engaged business, and it's a mild ranking factor in local search.</p>

<p>Second, potential customers read your responses. A thoughtful, professional response to a positive review reinforces trust. A professional, non-defensive response to a negative review can actually win customers who were on the fence.</p>

<p>For positive reviews, a simple personal response works:</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid var(--blue); padding: 12px 20px; margin: 24px 0; color: var(--dark); font-style: italic;">
  "Thank you so much, [Name]! It was a pleasure working on your [project]. We appreciate you taking the time to share your experience; call us anytime!"
</blockquote>

<p>For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge their concern, and offer to make it right offline. Never argue in a public response.</p>

<h2>Display Reviews on Your Website</h2>

<p>Reviews you earn on Google can and should be featured on your website. Copy three to five of your best reviews onto your homepage or a dedicated testimonials page. They don't need to be live-updating widgets; a simple static display with the customer's name and a star graphic is enough.</p>

<p>Reviews on your website serve a different purpose than reviews on Google: they're for visitors who are already on your site and deciding whether to call. The more social proof you can show in the moments before someone picks up the phone, the better your conversion rate.</p>

<h2>The Compound Effect</h2>

<p>Reviews build on themselves. Once you have 20+ recent, quality reviews, potential customers see you as the obvious choice; not because of your marketing but because of social proof from real customers. That's more powerful than any ad you can buy.</p>

<p>And once you have that review volume, Google starts ranking you higher for more searches, which brings in more customers, who leave more reviews. The system feeds itself.</p>

<p>It starts with one ask. Build the habit of asking after every job, and your review count will grow steadily without you having to think about it.</p>
