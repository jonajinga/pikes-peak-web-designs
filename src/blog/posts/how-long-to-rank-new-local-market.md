<p>Every service business owner asks me on the first call: how long until I rank? The answer is longer than sales pitches promise, shorter than skeptics warn, and predictable once you know what's happening at each stage.</p>

<p>Here's the timeline for a new website in a new local market, with the milestones that show your search work (SEO, or search engine optimization) is paying off before the rankings appear.</p>

<h2>The premise: a brand-new domain, freshly launched</h2>

<p>The timeline assumes:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A new domain with no history: no bought expired domain and no rankings carried over from an old site.</li>
  <li>A moderately competitive city, not New York and not a town of 4,000.</li>
  <li>A site built right from day one, with local content, structured data (code that describes the business to Google), the same name, address, and phone (NAP) everywhere, a submitted sitemap, and a Google Business Profile.</li>
  <li>One or two well-researched articles a month, plus occasional updates to the service-area pages.</li>
  <li>No paid ads, only unpaid ("organic") local search.</li>
</ul>

<p>If any of these differ, the timeline shifts, usually by weeks rather than months.</p>

<h2>Week 1: indexing begins</h2>

<p>The first week is mostly invisible. The sitemap goes to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, Google's crawler follows links from the homepage through the site, and Google verifies the Google Business Profile if it was just claimed.</p>

<p>By the end of week one, expect:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The homepage in Google's index (its catalog of pages), which a <code>site:yourdomain.com</code> search confirms.</li>
  <li>Two to four other pages found, perhaps not yet indexed.</li>
  <li>The profile marked "active," with few or no reviews yet.</li>
  <li>No measurable search traffic yet.</li>
</ul>

<p>Nothing is wrong; the search engines are still exploring. Resist the urge to "make something happen" with spam or shortcut links.</p>

<h2>Weeks 2-3: discovery completes</h2>

<p>By the end of week three, Google has usually indexed every page in the sitemap, or nearly all. For most searches, though, they rank nowhere visible yet. New pages usually start between position 50 and 200, indexed but not trusted enough to show near the top.</p>

<p>Expect:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Search Console showing 60 to 90 percent of submitted pages as "indexed."</li>
  <li>The first impressions in Search Console's Performance report: searches where the site appeared, even far down.</li>
  <li>The profile appearing for searches on your business name ("Acme Plumbing Colorado Springs" brings up the right business).</li>
  <li>A few search visits, almost all from people searching your name.</li>
</ul>

<p>Your own name comes first because it's the easiest thing for Google to confirm. When a search for "Acme Plumbing" puts acmeplumbing.com on top, Google's confidence in the site grows.</p>

<h2>Weeks 4-6: rankings for specific searches begin</h2>

<p>Around weeks four to six, searches that don't include your name start producing impressions. They're usually long-tail searches, meaning longer, specific questions with little competition: "tankless water heater installer Colorado Springs," "asphalt shingle vs metal roof Colorado climate," or "what does a sewer scope inspection cost." A thoughtful page can rank for these quickly.</p>

<p>This first traffic, perhaps 20 to 50 search visitors a week, won't look like much, but those visitors usually arrive ready to hire.</p>

<p>Expect by week six:</p>

<ul>
  <li>5 to 15 specific searches producing weekly impressions in Search Console.</li>
  <li>One to three of them with the site in the top 10 (page one, even if near the bottom).</li>
  <li>The first one to three clicks a week from search.</li>
  <li>The profile in the map results for some low-competition local searches, especially once it has three or more reviews.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Months 2-3: the local pack begins to respond</h2>

<p>Around 60 to 90 days, most well-built local service sites start showing up meaningfully in Google's map results (the "local pack"). The site has earned some trust, and the profile has enough reviews and activity to compete for the top three spots on some searches.</p>

<p>Expect by month three:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The profile in the map results for your main service-plus-city searches ("plumber Colorado Springs," or "roofer near me" within a few miles of your address).</li>
  <li>The homepage and main service pages in the top 20 to 30 for your main local keywords (page two or three).</li>
  <li>Specific searches bringing 50 to 150 visits a month.</li>
  <li>The first calls or form submissions you can trace to search.</li>
</ul>

<p>Search now produces leads you can count. It isn't at full speed, but from here the results build on each other.</p>

<h2>Months 4-6: organic ranks climb into top 10</h2>

<p>In months four to six, your main service searches move from page two to page one. Steady publishing has shown Google your expertise, the site has earned links from directories, local mentions, and the occasional partner, and the profile has enough reviews to contend in the map results.</p>

<p>Expect by month six:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Main service-plus-city searches in the top 10 most weeks.</li>
  <li>Map-result appearances for most relevant searches near your address.</li>
  <li>200 to 500 search visits a month, depending on your city's size and competition.</li>
  <li>Enough leads from search to justify the cost, even if it isn't yet your biggest source.</li>
</ul>

<p>From here, each new article or service page adds links and depth to the site, so new pieces bring results faster than the early ones did.</p>

<h2>Months 6-12: compounding kicks in</h2>

<p>In the second half of year one, search turns from a cost into a source of steady work. The site has dozens of indexed pages, hundreds of specific-search rankings, and a profile that's a default choice in the map results. New content ranks faster and lifts the pages around it.</p>

<p>Expect by month twelve:</p>

<ul>
  <li>500 to 1,500 or more search visits a month, depending on your city and publishing schedule.</li>
  <li>Several page-one rankings for your main service searches.</li>
  <li>Map-result appearances for almost every relevant search in your service area.</li>
  <li>Search as your largest single source of traffic, often more than direct visits, social media, and referrals combined.</li>
  <li>A steady, predictable flow of leads you can plan around.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What slows the timeline</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Inconsistent NAP across the web.</strong> Old listings with wrong addresses or phone numbers slow your climb. Fix them once, then check them quarterly.</li>
  <li><strong>Thin or copied content.</strong> City pages that differ only by the city name are judged low quality and never rank well. Each one needs its own content.</li>
  <li><strong>No publishing schedule.</strong> A site that launches with 15 pages and stops ranks more slowly than one that adds two good pages a month, because Google rewards steady work.</li>
  <li><strong>No reviews.</strong> Three reviews at launch is fine; three reviews six months later is a problem. Asking your customers for reviews is the easiest, most effective step in local search.</li>
  <li><strong>A new domain for a new business.</strong> If the business is as new as the site, Google has less outside evidence that you're established. Add three to six months.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What accelerates it</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>An established local reputation.</strong> Years of local standing (Chamber membership, a BBB rating, reviews on Yelp or Google) carry over to the new site faster than a cold start.</li>
  <li><strong>An older domain.</strong> A domain owned for years carries some trust, even if the old site was poor.</li>
  <li><strong>A useful blog from day one.</strong> Three or four substantial articles at launch, not filler, bring specific-search rankings within weeks instead of months.</li>
  <li><strong>Directory listings (citations) from the start.</strong> Consistent listings in the top 10 to 15 local directories on launch day speed your climb by several weeks.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The summary</h2>

<p>For a well-built site in a moderately competitive market without paid ads, expect meaningful search traffic by month three, steady leads by month six, and a strong place in the map results by month twelve. Faster promises usually cost more for the same result on a weaker footing, and "you can't rank without ads" is advice from before modern local search.</p>

<p>The biggest variable is a small monthly routine: one or two new pieces of content, a few new reviews, and a quarterly check of your listings. Sites that keep it up end year one well ahead, and the gains last.</p>
