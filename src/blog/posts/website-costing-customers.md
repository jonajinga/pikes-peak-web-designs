<p>You check your website now and then, and it seems fine. The pages load, your phone number is there, and the pictures look okay.</p>

<p>But "fine" is a low bar. A website that doesn't turn visitors into phone calls is about as useful as no website at all. It costs you hosting fees every month and earns none of it back.</p>

<p>Here are five signs your current website is working against you.</p>

<h2>1. It Takes More Than 3 Seconds to Load</h2>

<p>Speed matters, especially for mobile users, who now make up most local service searches. Google's research shows that 53% of mobile visitors leave a site that takes longer than three seconds to load.</p>

<p>Most WordPress and do-it-yourself builder sites are much slower than they look. Large images, heavy plugins, slow shared hosting, and poorly written theme code all add up, so a site that looks fine on your office computer can crawl on a phone.</p>

<p>How to check: open Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool and enter your website address. If your mobile score is below 70, you have a serious problem.</p>

<h2>2. It Doesn't Look Right on a Phone</h2>

<p>Pull up your website on your own phone right now. Can you read the text without zooming? Are the buttons easy to tap? Does tapping the phone number open the dialer?</p>

<p>If any answer is "no" or "sort of," you're losing mobile customers, and that's most of them. Over 60% of searches for local service businesses happen on mobile devices. A site that doesn't work well on a phone is worse than inconvenient. It's invisible, because Google ranks pages that aren't mobile-friendly lower in its results.</p>

<h2>3. It's Hard to Find Your Phone Number</h2>

<p>This sounds obvious, but many small business sites bury the phone number in the footer or show it only on the Contact page. Someone with a leaking pipe or a tripped breaker wants to call right away, and they won't hunt for your number.</p>

<p>Your phone number should be:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Visible in the header on every page</li>
  <li>Tappable on phones (a <code>tel:</code> link)</li>
  <li>Prominent in the top section of your home page</li>
  <li>Repeated near every button and contact form</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. It Looks Outdated or Untrustworthy</h2>

<p>Homeowners decide whether to trust you quickly, often within a few seconds of landing on your site. If the design looks dated, the photos are grainy stock images, or the whole site looks like it was built in 2012, visitors make an unspoken guess about the quality of your work.</p>

<p>That isn't fair, but it happens. A clean, professional website signals a professional business and turns a cautious visitor into a call.</p>

<p>Signs your site may be giving the wrong impression:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Tiny fonts, or text that runs the full width of a desktop screen</li>
  <li>Clip art or blurry images</li>
  <li>Clashing colors or no clear order on the page</li>
  <li>No mention of licenses, insurance, or certifications</li>
  <li>No customer reviews or testimonials anywhere</li>
</ul>

<h2>5. You're Not Showing Up on Google for Local Searches</h2>

<p>Search for your own services right now: "plumber Colorado Springs," "HVAC repair Fountain CO," or "roofer near Woodland Park." Do you show up?</p>

<p>If you're not on page one, and ideally in the top three, many potential customers will never find you, however good your work is. Local search ranking (local SEO) is built into your website itself: its page titles, headings, content, and code. A site without it is close to invisible to the people most likely to hire you.</p>

<p>This is one of the biggest gaps I see in sites built on do-it-yourself platforms or low-cost WordPress themes. They look fine, but Google can't tell what they're about or where the business works, so they rank poorly for the searches that matter.</p>

<h2>What a Working Website Looks Like</h2>

<p>A strong small business website doesn't need to be complicated. It needs to be fast, clear, trustworthy, and easy to find, and it needs to tell visitors what you do, where you do it, and how to reach you.</p>

<p>If your site shows any of the five problems above, the good news is that none of them is hard to fix, as long as you're willing to replace the site rather than patch it. Patching a slow, dated WordPress site usually produces a slightly less slow, slightly less dated one.</p>

<p>Your website should be making you money. If it isn't, it's time to change that.</p>
