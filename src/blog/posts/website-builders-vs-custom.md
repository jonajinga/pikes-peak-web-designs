<p>Website builders such as Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, and Weebly advertise heavily to small businesses. Their pitch is simple: build your own website in minutes for a low monthly fee, with no technical skills.</p>

<p>For a personal portfolio or a bakery selling gift cards, that pitch holds up. For a service business that depends on Google searches and phone calls, the picture is more complicated.</p>

<h2>What Website Builders Are Good At</h2>

<p>Website builders have clear strengths:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Low starting cost (typically $15 to $40 a month for a basic plan)</li>
  <li>No technical knowledge needed to get started</li>
  <li>Drag-and-drop editors that make layout changes easy</li>
  <li>Built-in hosting with reasonable uptime</li>
  <li>Templates that look modern enough</li>
</ul>

<p>If all you need is a digital business card, something to point people to when they ask whether you have a website, a Wix or Squarespace site does that job.</p>

<h2>Where They Fall Short for Service Businesses</h2>

<h3>Performance and Page Speed</h3>

<p>Website builders produce heavy code. Every platform loads its own extras on every page (scripts, style frameworks, tracking, and add-on features), whether your site needs them or not, and that slows pages down.</p>

<p>Wix in particular has a history of low scores on Google's PageSpeed Insights, often 30 to 55 on mobile. As the post on page speed explains, slow pages hold down your Google ranking and raise your bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave without doing anything).</p>

<h3>SEO Limitations</h3>

<p>All three major builders have improved their basic SEO (search engine optimization) tools, and you can set page titles and descriptions, which is the minimum. But some problems are built in:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The platform often sets page addresses and site structure, and not always well</li>
  <li>The code is heavy and harder for Google to read than custom-coded HTML</li>
  <li>Schema markup (structured data that tells Google what your business does) is limited or costs extra</li>
  <li>Wix has a history of trouble showing JavaScript content to search engines</li>
</ul>

<p>For a business that gets most of its revenue from Google searches, these are serious problems.</p>

<h3>Who Controls Your Site</h3>

<p>A Wix or Squarespace site lives on the platform's servers, in its own format. If the platform raises prices, changes its terms, or closes, your site is affected, and you can't move it to another host.</p>

<p>On my plan, your domain is registered in your name and you control it, while I own and look after the code. If you cancel, I help you move the domain to a registrar you choose, and the site goes offline at the end of the term.</p>

<h3>The "Low Cost" Math Doesn't Always Work Out</h3>

<p>Squarespace Business is $33 a month, Wix Business is around $27 a month, and GoDaddy's website and marketing plan runs $25 to $45 a month. None of these includes a custom domain (add $15 to $20 a year) or the premium features you'll likely need, such as online sales, booking, or more storage.</p>

<p>Add those, plus the hours you'll spend building and maintaining the site yourself, and the savings shrink.</p>

<h2>GoDaddy's Done-for-You Option</h2>

<p>GoDaddy also offers a "professional website" service that builds the site for you, which is appealing and fairly affordable. But you're still tied to GoDaddy's platform, the sites are templates with little customization, and the search setup is basic.</p>

<p>I regularly talk with contractors who spent $500 to $1,500 on a GoDaddy "done for you" site and got no phone calls from it. The site exists, but it doesn't rank.</p>

<h2>What a Custom-Coded Site Does Differently</h2>

<p>A custom-coded website is written from scratch for your business, so every line of code has a job. The result:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Core Web Vitals (Google's page-experience measures) in the green on every page</li>
  <li>Scores of 95 to 100 on Google's speed test (PageSpeed)</li>
  <li>Clean HTML that Google reads easily</li>
  <li>Service-area and service pages built around how people search</li>
  <li>Your domain in your name, under your control</li>
</ul>

<p>It's built to bring in leads.</p>

<h2>When a Website Builder Is Fine</h2>

<p>If most of your customers come from word of mouth, referrals, or social media, and you need only a basic online presence, a website builder is a perfectly reasonable choice. Not every business needs a site built to win search rankings.</p>

<p>But if Google is a big part of how customers find you, or how you want them to, a builder's speed and search limits will cost you leads. Lost customers add up faster than most people expect.</p>

<p>So the question isn't whether a custom site costs more each month, but whether the customers it brings in cover the cost. For most service businesses with a steady flow of jobs, they do.</p>
