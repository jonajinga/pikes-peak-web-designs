<p>If you run a contracting business in Colorado Springs, whether in roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, or landscaping, you've heard that you need a website. You probably have one, or someone built you one a few years back.</p>

<p>The question worth asking is whether your website is working for you.</p>

<p>Having a website is different from having one that brings in phone calls, builds trust, and helps you close jobs. Most contractors I talk to have the first kind. This article is about the second.</p>

<h2>Why the Pikes Peak region is so competitive right now</h2>

<p>Colorado Springs has grown a great deal over the past decade. More people means more housing, and more housing means more demand for contractors, but also more competition. When a homeowner in Fountain or Woodland Park needs a roofer after a hailstorm, they open Google, not the Yellow Pages.</p>

<p>If you don't show up, or your site looks like it was built in 2009, that job goes to someone who invested in their website.</p>

<h2>What a working contractor website does</h2>

<p>A contractor website that works isn't complicated. It needs no animation, chatbot, or online store, just four things done well:</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Load fast.</strong> Most homeowners searching for an emergency plumber or storm repair are on a phone with a weak signal. If your site takes more than 3 seconds to load, they're gone.</li>
  <li><strong>Show up in local search.</strong> When someone searches "electrician Colorado Springs" or "HVAC repair Fountain CO," your site needs to appear. That takes proper search engine optimization (SEO) on each page and local signals built into the code.</li>
  <li><strong>Build trust at a glance.</strong> A clean, professional site tells a homeowner you run a professional business. A cluttered, outdated one says the opposite, even if you're the best contractor in El Paso County.</li>
  <li><strong>Make it easy to contact you.</strong> Put the phone number up front, add a tap-to-call button on phones, and keep the contact form short, so calling you takes no effort.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The cost of a bad website</h2>

<p>Contractors often think of a website as an expense. I'd encourage you to think of it as a salesperson who works around the clock, seven days a week, and never takes a sick day or asks for a raise.</p>

<p>One extra job a month, even a small one, more than pays for a professional website. If your site convinces one homeowner a month to call you instead of a competitor, that's thousands of dollars in extra revenue every year.</p>

<p>Now flip it around. If your site turns people away because it's slow, looks untrustworthy, or can't be found on Google, what is that costing you?</p>

<h2>Why WordPress and DIY builders often fall short for contractors</h2>

<p>I see two common situations: a WordPress site a relative built, or a site the contractor made on Wix or Squarespace. Both usually fall short.</p>

<p>WordPress works with steady upkeep, but without it, a site grows slow and open to security problems. Wix and Squarespace make it easy to get online, but their sites often look generic. They rarely rank well in local search, because they're built from templates and carry code you don't need.</p>

<p>What works is a custom-coded site, built for your business, your location, and your trade. That's what I build.</p>

<h2>What to look for in a contractor website</h2>

<p>Whether you work with me or someone else, your contractor website should have:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A clear headline that says what you do and where (for example, "Colorado Springs Roofing, Licensed and Insured Since 2008")</li>
  <li>A phone number you can see without scrolling, especially on a phone</li>
  <li>A list of your services with short descriptions</li>
  <li>Your service area: neighborhoods, cities, and ZIP codes</li>
  <li>Photos of your own work instead of stock images</li>
  <li>Customer reviews or testimonials</li>
  <li>Your license number and any certifications</li>
  <li>A simple contact form or quote request form</li>
</ul>

<p>If your current site is missing several of these, it's worth talking about what a new site could do for your business.</p>

<p>Colorado Springs homeowners search for contractors every day. Make sure yours is the site they find, and the one that convinces them to call.</p>
