<p>Electrical contracting runs on trust. A homeowner who calls an electrician is inviting a stranger in to work on systems that can burn the house down if they're done wrong. Your website often decides whether that trust goes to you or to a competitor.</p>

<p>Most electrician websites fail on the basics. They're slow, they don't say clearly what the company does and where, and they make it too hard to get in touch. Here's what works.</p>

<h2>Put your license and insurance front and center</h2>

<p>The biggest question homeowners and property managers ask when hiring an electrician is: <em>are they licensed and insured?</em> Unlicensed electrical work creates serious liability, because faulty work can fail inspection, void insurance policies, or start fires. Homeowners know this, even if they don't say it out loud.</p>

<p>So put your license number and insurance status on your home page, never in the fine print. Show them in the top section, the header, or a strip of trust signals, with wording like:</p>

<ul>
  <li>"Licensed Electrical Contractor, License #12345"</li>
  <li>"Fully Licensed and Insured, Residential and Commercial"</li>
  <li>"Master Electrician on every job"</li>
</ul>

<p>This builds instant trust and tells Google your content comes with relevant credentials, which helps you win calls and rank locally.</p>

<h2>Separate pages for residential and commercial</h2>

<p>Residential and commercial electrical work are different businesses with different buyers. A homeowner looking for a panel upgrade searches for different things than a property manager looking for commercial rewiring or a tenant build-out.</p>

<p>If you do both, give each its own service page. One page for both audiences weakens the message for each. A homeowner doesn't care about commercial load calculations, and a property manager doesn't care about bathroom fan replacements.</p>

<p>Separate pages let you:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Use the right language and concerns for each audience</li>
  <li>Rank for different sets of search terms (residential vs. commercial electrician)</li>
  <li>Offer a more relevant page that wins more calls for each kind of request</li>
</ul>

<h2>Service pages that target the terms people search</h2>

<p>Most electrician websites have one generic "Services" page that lists everything, which wastes a chance to rank in search (SEO, or search engine optimization). Every service you offer is a separate search. Someone who needs a new panel doesn't search "electrician services." They search "electrical panel upgrade [city]" or "200 amp service upgrade near me."</p>

<p>Build a page for each of your most valuable services:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Electrical panel upgrades and service upgrades</li>
  <li>EV charger installation</li>
  <li>Whole-home rewiring</li>
  <li>Generator installation and hookup</li>
  <li>Ceiling fan installation</li>
  <li>Outlet and switch replacement</li>
  <li>Outdoor lighting and landscape lighting</li>
  <li>Safety inspections and electrical assessments</li>
</ul>

<p>Each page should focus on its service, name your location, and end with a clear call to action. That's how smaller electrical companies outrank larger competitors in local search again and again.</p>

<h2>EV charger installation is a growth opportunity right now</h2>

<p>More people are buying electric vehicles every year, and "EV charger installation" is one of the fastest-growing searches in electrical contracting. Many new EV owners don't know whom to call, so they search Google and call whoever comes up first.</p>

<p>If you install EV chargers, you need a page built for this search. Include:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The brands you install (ChargePoint, Tesla Wall Connector, JuiceBox, and others)</li>
  <li>A plain explanation of Level 1 and Level 2 chargers for homeowners who don't know the difference</li>
  <li>Whether you handle permits (you should, and you should say so)</li>
  <li>Your price range or a "free assessment" offer</li>
  <li>Your service area</li>
</ul>

<p>For most electricians, this is a valuable job ($500 to $1,500 or more, depending on panel work), and the chance to win it through unpaid search is large.</p>

<h2>Make emergency service easy to find</h2>

<p>Electrical emergencies happen at night, on weekends, and on holidays. A breaker that won't reset, a burning smell from the panel, or outlets that stop working all send people searching in a panic for an electrician who can come now.</p>

<p>If you offer emergency or same-day service, put your phone number at the very top of the page, where visitors see it without scrolling. On phones, show it in large type, ideally as a tap-to-call link, instead of tucking it into the footer or a contact page.</p>

<p>A site that puts the phone number three scrolls down loses emergency calls to competitors who make calling easy. It's one of the best-paying fixes you can make, and one of the four levers the <a href="/roi/" class="inline-link">return-on-investment page</a> works through for a typical local service business.</p>

<h2>Photos of your work build trust, even simple ones</h2>

<p>Electrical work is hard to photograph impressively, but any photo of your own jobs beats a stock shot of a smiling electrician in a hard hat. Homeowners can tell the difference, and your own photos show a working company with finished jobs.</p>

<p>Good photos to take and use:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A clean, newly installed panel with proper labels</li>
  <li>A newly installed EV charger in a garage</li>
  <li>Finished outdoor or landscape lighting on one of your projects</li>
  <li>Your truck in front of a job site</li>
  <li>Your crew, or even a simple headshot of the owner, which goes a long way</li>
</ul>

<p>Phone photos taken in good light work fine, so you don't need a professional photographer. You need photos taken on every job, every time.</p>

<h2>Google reviews for electricians: what to ask for</h2>

<p>Electrical work suits review requests well. Customers often don't understand what went into the job, but they know you fixed the problem quickly, explained it clearly, and left no mess. Those stories make great Google reviews.</p>

<p>Within 24 hours of every job, follow up by text or email with a direct link to your Google review page. Here's a sample message:</p>

<blockquote style="border-left:3px solid var(--blue);padding:12px 20px;margin:24px 0;font-style:italic;color:var(--text-muted);">"Hi [Name], thanks for trusting us with your electrical work today. If you have a moment, we'd appreciate a Google review. It helps families in [City] find a licensed electrician they can trust. Here's the link: [link]"</blockquote>

<p>Personalize it a little for each customer. A follow-up like this gets three to five times more reviews than hoping customers will leave one on their own.</p>

<h2>Make your service area explicit</h2>

<p>Electricians usually serve a set radius, often 30 to 50 miles from their shop or home base. Yet most electrician websites are vague about where they work. "Serving the greater metro area" doesn't tell a homeowner or property manager whether you serve their neighborhood.</p>

<p>List the cities you serve on your home page, About page, and contact page. If you cover several counties or a wide radius, create location pages for the major cities and suburbs. Each one can target "[city] electrician" searches and help you rank across a wider area.</p>

<h2>The most common electrician website mistakes</h2>

<p>Looking at electrician websites across the country, these are the mistakes I see most often, and they cost leads:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Phone number hidden on mobile.</strong> If the phone number isn't in the header as a tap-to-call link, you're losing emergency calls.</li>
  <li><strong>No mention of license or insurance.</strong> It's the top trust signal for electrical work, so put it on the home page.</li>
  <li><strong>One "Services" page and no individual service pages.</strong> Every service is a separate search, so you need separate pages.</li>
  <li><strong>No photos of your own work.</strong> Stock photos don't win calls, and job photos do.</li>
  <li><strong>No mention of EV charger installation.</strong> It's a growing source of revenue with strong demand in search.</li>
  <li><strong>Slow pages.</strong> Google ranks slow sites lower in local search. If your site takes more than 3 seconds to load, you're losing rankings.</li>
</ul>
