# Copy style guide

Approved by Jon Ajinga on October 29, 2026. Every visitor-facing page on pikespeakwebdesigns.com follows these rules. They rest on Strunk and White's *The Elements of Style*: plain, warm, and client-friendly.

## Voice

1. **One writer, one reader.** Write as "I" to "you". When a noun is needed, say "Pikes Peak Web Designs". Never "the agency", "our team", "our", or "studio".
2. **"We" means you and me.** Use "we" only for work Jon and the client do together. Everything Jon does alone is "I".
3. **Jon appears as a person.** Use his first name on key pages, with short personal reasons where they help, such as why the veterans credit exists. No long stories.
4. **Courteous and plain.** Friendly, polite, and direct, like a good letter to a customer. No slang, jokes, sarcasm, or exclamation marks.
5. **Contractions throughout.** Write "don't", "I'll", and "you're". Keep the full form only for emphasis, as in "I do not take referral kickbacks".

## Sentences and paragraphs

6. **Moderate length.** Sentences average 16 to 18 words and rarely run past 30. Paragraphs hold one point, stated first, in no more than four sentences.
7. **Active voice and positive form.** Name who does what: "I send you a written estimate." Say what the service does. Keep a "no" only where the absence is itself the benefit ($0 down, no cookie banner), and use at most one list of "no"s per page.
8. **Omit needless words.** Cut "actually", "genuinely", "simply", "quietly", "really", "tend to", "in short order", "along the way", "with that said", and "in the background".
9. **Show honesty; don't announce it.** Drop "honest", "real" used for emphasis, and "this is not a sales pitch".
10. **Plain words for business things.** Write "approach" (not "posture"), "working together" (not "engagement"), "launch" or "publish" (not "ship"), "schedule" (not "cadence"), and "site" (not "deliverable"). Drop "surface", "pipeline", and "dunning". Honor the banned-word list in `src/_data/site.js`.
11. **Parallel form, emphasis at the end.** Items in a list, and headings in a set, share one grammatical shape. End sentences and headings on the point, not on a negation or a qualifier.

## Technical terms

12. **Plain meaning first.** On sales pages, explain in everyday words and give the trade term once in parentheses: "Google's map results (the 'local pack')".
13. **Tool names stay on the technical pages.** Cloudflare, Umami, Web3Forms, Pagefind, and Lighthouse are named only on Tools I Use and Technical Approach. Sales pages say what each tool does.
14. **"Custom-coded" is the signature term.** Explain it once near the top of the home page: "I write each site from scratch instead of filling in a template." Don't use "static site" or "static export" on sales pages.
15. **Split by audience.** What I Build is fully plain. All technical detail lives on Technical Approach.
16. **Badges pair plain words with the proof.** "95–100 on Google's speed test" · "Meets WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility".

## Claims and numbers

17. **Reply times.** Say "quickly on most business days" once per page at most.
18. **Numbers.** Use figures for money, percentages, scores, and anything 10 or above; spell out one to nine in running text. Write "$175 a month", never "one hundred and seventy-five dollars".
19. **ROI figures are an example.** Label them as the worked example for a typical local business.
20. **One fact, one wording.** Use the approved facts below, worded the same way everywhere.
21. **Say it once, link to the rest.** Each fact has one home page; other pages give one sentence and a link.

## Headings

22. **No period** at the end of a heading.
23. **Page titles keep their two parts.** The italic second half states the main benefit, never a negation.
24. **"The honest math"** becomes "What one extra call a week is worth".
25. **The Service Site Standard** keeps its name on its own page. Elsewhere, name the principle instead of citing its number.
26. **"Boring is the feature."** is retired.
27. **The top bar and footer line** is the tagline: "A website you never have to worry about".

## Calls to action

28. **Main button: "Start now"**, which opens the sign-up steps. The call is the second choice, as a text link: "Prefer to talk first? Book a free call", with "20 to 30 minutes on Google Meet" beside it.
29. **Closing blocks carry one button and one link.**
30. **Button labels** start with a verb, use sentence case, and never begin with "Or".

## Competitors

31. **Mention other platforms** only on the eight comparison pages and in one section of the home page.
32. **On comparison pages, be fair.** Open with what the platform does well, then the trade-offs. No loaded words such as "mediocrity", "template mill", or "quietly".

## Punctuation and emphasis

33. **Use the serial comma.** Never join two sentences with a comma. Use a semicolon only between two complete sentences, and a colon before a list or an explanation. Write "and", not "+" or "&", in running text.
34. **No em dashes.** Rebuild each sentence the old dash-removal script damaged with commas, colons, parentheses, or a full stop.
35. **No bold sentences inside paragraphs.** Bold only the labels at the start of list items.
36. **Proof cards.** Three on the home page (demo sites, speed scores, the agreement); all six on About.

## Length

37. **Cut the main pages by 30 to 40 percent**, by removing filler and repetition. Every claim stays somewhere on the site.

## Approved facts

- **Price.** $175 a month, $0 down, a 12-month minimum, then month to month with 30 days' written notice.
- **Plans.** One plan only. No lump-sum option on the public site.
- **Discovery call.** Free, 20 to 30 minutes, on Google Meet, and optional: anyone may sign up without one.
- **Build time.** Two to three weeks.
- **Build start.** Work begins when the first payment clears.
- **Extra work.** An extra page beyond the ten included costs $100 per page. Other work outside the plan costs $100 an hour, with a written estimate first.
- **Logos.** Jon does not design logos.
- **Ownership.** The client's domain is registered in the client's name, and the client controls it. On the monthly plan, Jon owns the code. At cancellation, Jon helps move the domain to a registrar of the client's choice and the website goes offline at the end of the term. There is no export of the site and no code buyout.
- **Case studies.** There are no paid-client case studies yet. Don't promise "real-client before-and-after numbers".
- **Agreement.** Describe it as a plain-English service agreement, not a "one-page" agreement; it has 12 sections.
